# Optional payment detection and spending alerts

Payment detection is off initially. In Settings, choose a destination budget and the payment apps you want to enable, grant notification access in Android settings, then explicitly enable detection. The supported package allowlist is PhonePe (`com.phonepe.app`), Google Pay (`com.google.android.apps.nbu.paisa.user`), and Paytm (`net.one97.paytm`). Changing the selected budget elsewhere in the app does not redirect imports. Enabling access does not scan old notifications.

If Android blocks notification access with a restricted-settings message after installing the downloaded APK, open Android Settings → Apps → DayBudget → More → Allow restricted settings, then return to notification access. Menu names can vary by phone. Grant this only if you choose to trust the app with notification access; manual budgets work without it. [Google's restricted-settings instructions](https://support.google.com/android/answer/12623953?hl=en)

Leave automatic recording off if you already enter purchases manually. A manual entry has no payment reference, so it cannot reliably be matched to an incoming notification. Check the inbox and existing entries before accepting a detected payment. Review lets you change the amount, merchant, date, note, and destination before its first import attempt. After an attempt, retry stays in that destination to prevent a restart from counting the same payment in two budgets.

Automatic recording requires its own toggle, a recognized outgoing-payment pattern, one valid positive amount, a merchant name, and one transaction reference. Recognized English patterns include `Paid ₹200 to Tea Shop. UPI transaction ID: …`, `Sent INR 200 to Tea Shop. UTR: …`, and `₹200 paid to Tea Shop. Transaction ID: …`. These are examples of accepted patterns, not a claim that every current version of those apps uses them. A missing reference or merchant requires review. Conflicting, malformed, or zero amounts are skipped. Requests, pending/failed payments, credits, refunds, OTPs, rewards, transfers, self/own-account payments, and ATM withdrawals are excluded when those meanings are stated in the notification. Unknown wording may be skipped, and a template cannot establish the real purpose of every transfer. Correct any misclassified entry in the app.

Enter refunds manually and link them to the original expense when appropriate. Do not enter an own-account transfer or ATM withdrawal as a purchase simply because money left an account. Detection does not reconcile a bank statement or establish that a payment settled. Other languages, changed templates, notifications suppressed by the payment app, missing text, work-profile restrictions, and operating-system restrictions can prevent detection. Android 15 also restricts sensitive notification content such as OTPs. [Android notification-listener documentation](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/service/notification/NotificationListenerService), [Android 15 privacy changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/15/behavior-changes-all)

Android's notification-access screen grants broad system access. DayBudget checks the source package against the enabled allowlist before accessing message fields. It retains only extracted amount, merchant, source package, event time/date, target budget, keyed reference/notification identifiers, and import/review receipts. It does not save raw notification text, account numbers, UPI addresses, or raw payment references. Identifiers use HMAC-SHA256 with a random app-private key that survives restart. Missing keys or unreadable capture receipts stop detection rather than silently resetting duplicate protection. No SMS, bank API, network permission, analytics, or cloud service is used. Local capture facts and decisions remain until Delete everything, which also disables detection and removes the private key. Ignoring a candidate or deleting an imported expense retains duplicate protection during normal use.

Saved-expense notifications and overspending alerts are separate optional settings. On Android 13 and later, displaying them also requires the app's notification permission; listener access alone does not grant it. An overspending alert is attempted at most once per budget per day when a newly committed expense leaves today's remaining amount below zero. All spending alerts are quiet from **22:00 until 08:00 in that budget's timezone**. Quiet-hour alerts are not queued for later playback. Ordinary saved-expense notifications can continue outside quiet hours when enabled. [Android notification permission](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/notifications/notification-permission)

The overspending channel starts at high importance with the system's default alarm tone, using notification audio settings. Sound remains subject to notification volume, channel settings, Do Not Disturb, permission denial, and system suppression. It does not force volume, bypass Do Not Disturb, loop an alarm, or open a full-screen alert. Android owns channel behavior after creation; use the channel-settings buttons to change it. [Android notification channels](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/notifications/channels)

Automated parser tests use synthetic text. Controller tests feed synthetic `StatusBarNotification` objects through the listener callback path with simulated consent and isolated local databases. They check filtering, review, restart recovery, duplicate protection, edits/deletion, and destination restrictions. They do not certify real PhonePe, Google Pay, or Paytm templates, actual account transactions, audible output, or every device's notification delivery. Device and manual results are reported separately in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md).
